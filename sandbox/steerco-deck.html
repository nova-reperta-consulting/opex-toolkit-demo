Motor claims: getting the time back

20 October 2026
Northwind Assurance · Steering committee · OpEx Quickscan

Over a third of Claims Handling's 42 FTE is avoidable, mostly moving information, and five fixes would free 10,0 FTE in the claim flow

Ten analyses in September covered Claims Handling's 42 FTE and the claim work in two neighbouring teams

  • Five activity blocks and 24 activities mapped against one operating baseline
  • Customer Service and Finance Control included where they touch the motor claim
  • Volumes stable at about 1.900 notifications a month, and the team agrees with the headcount

14,9 of the 42 FTE is avoidable, and almost all known loss is information re-typed, looked up or chased

  1. 1Chasing adjuster reports (3,0 FTE), re-typing the notification (2,5) and looking up cover (1,8)
  2. 2Customers ring because they cannot see where their claim stands: 4,0 FTE in Customer Service
  3. 3Only 32% of available time adds value, and nothing records who can handle which claim type

Five improvements would release 10,0 FTE, and three system fixes carry 5,3 of it

  1. 1Straight-through notification: contact-centre link, cover on the claim, adjuster due dates tracked (5,3 FTE)Automation · 8 weeks · Head of Claims Operations
  2. 2No status call to make: tell customers what happened before they ask (3,2 of the 4,0 FTE)AI Enhance · 10 weeks · Customer Service Manager
  3. 3Settlement and triage redesign: settle inside the claim (1,5 FTE) and triage on skill (not yet sized)Process redesign · 6 weeks · Claims Handling Manager

While performance data is timely and trusted, no team routine uses it to steer the work: seven of fourteen practices score weak

Performance wheel of Claims Handling, five dimensions, scores 1 to 5

1Mission & Vision2.5/5
Is the mission & vision clear?
  • Strategy clarity 3/5: Handlers describe their job as clearing the queue. None of the four interviewed connected their work to a service promise made to the customer.
  • Line of sight 2/5: The department objectives are published once a year on the intranet and are not referred to again.
5Consequences & Recognition2.3/5
Do we act, reward & recognise?
  • Follow-through on actions 2/5: Of eleven actions logged since April, the team lead could confirm three as closed.
  • Recognition 3/5: Good work is acknowledged warmly and in person, and handlers said so unprompted.
  • Development link 2/5: Training is offered when a handler asks, and the budget was underspent last year.
4Performance Dialogues2.7/5
Do we discuss performance?
  • Team routine 2/5: There is no standing team meeting. Information moves by email and by walking over.
  • One-to-ones 3/5: The team lead holds a monthly one-to-one with each handler and they are valued.
  • Escalation 3/5: Complex claims have a clear escalation route to the technical referent and it works.
2Metrics & Objectives2.3/5
Translated into metrics & targets?
  • Metric relevance 3/5: Volume, ageing and settlement time are tracked per claim type and are the right three to watch.
  • Target setting 2/5: Targets are set for the team as a whole and carried over from last year without being re-argued.
  • Cascade to individuals 2/5: No handler has an individual objective tied to a team metric.
3Performance Tracking3.0/5
Do we measure & visualise them?
  • Visual management 3/5: It is not referred to in any meeting, and the coordinators could not say who owns it.
  • Data timeliness 4/5: The claims system refreshes overnight and the numbers were trusted by everyone interviewed.
  • Workload visibility 2/5: Work is allocated by pulling from a shared queue, so nobody can see who is carrying what.
Source: interviews with the team lead, two coordinators and handlers, 3–4 Sep 2026 Good 4–5Fair 3Weak 1–2

Where accountability breaks

Triage: nobody decides

The coordinator and the manager each say the other decides, so complex files go to whoever is free rather than to who can handle them

Settlement: two signatures

Recovery: two teams, no owner
